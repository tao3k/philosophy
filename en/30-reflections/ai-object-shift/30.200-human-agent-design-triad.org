* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.30.200-human-agent-design-triad-en
:END:
When one sentence can book a ticket while coding and design still need canvases, files, diffs and running results, how
can we leave both narrow visions behind: that every interface will disappear, and that AI is merely another feature
inside an interface? How should human, agent and design form a new relation, and where should interaction offer less or
more?
* Argument
Interface and natural language are not two product forms competing to become the only entrance. Together with agent
action, they carry different needs as a matter is expressed, performed, perceived and changed in common. The question
is how they compose, not which one is declared the replacement in advance.
** Traditional Object and Applicability Boundary
Traditional apps treat interface as a capability catalog. Buttons, menus, windows and pages both tell people what the
system can do and prescribe the steps. The main design object is the directly operating human; good design makes
function discoverable, action learnable and state responsive.

This experience does not expire with AI. Human vision perceives position, structure, proportion and difference in
parallel. A stable canvas can become something several people point to, discuss and change together. The traditional
limit is binding “a person must see this” to “a person must operate every step,” while treating software's prior feature
division as the natural starting point.
** Object Shift
Purpose can now appear first in natural language, and an agent translates it into search, tool calls and action across
services. In many errands a person need not browse capability catalogs. State origin, time, budget and what matters;
the agent can search, compare, contact support and reserve.

Other activities are not execution of an already known purpose. Writing, programming, drawing, architecture and
research form their questions through action. People need to see several objects at once, change one directly and
observe how the whole responds. Visualization here is not packaging around an operating burden; it is where thought
and creation happen. Codex-like work is not a dialogue alone: files, code, diffs, terminals, tests and review let person
and agent think around the same changing work.

The object does not simply change from human to agent. A triadic relation forms. The person brings purpose, experience,
judgment and aesthetic sense. The agent interprets context, unfolds possibilities, coordinates capabilities and acts.
Design organizes a space in which both can see, change and correct together. “Design” is not a third personality but
the form that makes this relation possible.
** Judgment
The present judgment is that future interaction is neither GUI-first nor chat-only. It moves among language, agent
action and visible shared objects according to cognitive and practical need. Natural language is often the fastest
entrance for purpose. An agent often carries cross-system execution with least effort. Visualization is often fastest
for comparing complex relations, shaping directly, reviewing together and retaining long context.

“What should be less” cannot be decided by minimalist taste alone; remove operating burden unrelated to the person's
purpose. “What should be more” cannot be decided by feature accumulation; fully show what helps people form judgment,
notice difference, feel a work, understand consequence and work with others. Room and richness are not enemies. Room
keeps the irrelevant from occupying life. Richness keeps what deserves attention from being flattened.

#+EXPLANATION: The three do not have fixed jobs; they recombine according to how a task forms purpose, changes reality and receives judgment.
| Position in the relation | Better suited to carry | Must not be forced to carry | What it needs from the other two |
|--------------------------+------------------------+-----------------------------+----------------------------------|
| Human | Form and revise purposes; feel tradeoffs; make aesthetic and ethical judgments | Remember every entrance, repeat forms and monitor all execution detail | Agent carries tedium; design makes decisive relations visible and changeable |
| Agent | Interpret speech, search, translate, coordinate, propose candidates and fulfill entrustment | Pretend to exhaust human purpose or monopolize interpretation and authority | Person supplies revocable direction; design supplies clear state and shared objects |
| Design | Organize attention, juxtapose difference, form manipulable common things, connect action to effect | Fix every path or use visual polish to hide power | Human experience corrects form; agent capability enters when needed without occupying all |

** What becomes less and what becomes more

What may diminish is navigation that exists for software's internal structure: switching apps, repeated sign-in, feature
hunting, entering the same facts in different forms, waiting on support and tracking scattered notices. An agent can
handle these quietly and return only on exception or a genuine fork.

What should remain or become fuller are relations people grasp faster through perception and experience: distance on
a map, conflict on a timeline, exact change in a code diff, proportion on a canvas, the character of alternatives seen
together, linkage between budget and schedule, and the trace of several people's edits. An agent may generate and
explain these visible objects but should not compress them all into linear text.

#+NAME: interaction-form-by-work-en
#+EXPLANATION: Form follows the work; “fastest” means different media handing off across cognitive acts, not one medium always winning.
| Kind of work | Value of natural language | Value of agent action | Value of visible and direct manipulation |
|--------------+---------------------------+-----------------------+------------------------------------------|
| Clear-purpose errand such as booking | State constraints and concerns quickly | Search, compare, communicate, fill and prepare payment | Show a few candidates and effects at a decisive tradeoff or exception |
| Calendar and multi-person coordination | State relations, priorities and non-tradeable commitments | Find overlap, contact, wait for replies and maintain a draft | Timeline shows conflict, open time and who carries a change together |
| Coding | Describe problem, intent and acceptance | Search code, edit, run tests and explain failure | Files, diff, terminal and review form an inspectable work |
| Visual design | Describe mood, purpose, references and unformed ideas | Generate variants, arrange material and perform repeated edits | Canvas, proportion, color and space require juxtaposition and direct shaping |
| Long reflection on life | Tell stories, hesitation and value conflict | Organize clues, find real entrances and remember agreed relations | Show paths and comparison only when they aid understanding; do not turn life into a dashboard |

** A new interaction rhythm

New design should not begin by choosing either a chat interface or a visual workbench. It watches how the matter
unfolds. A sentence may begin; the agent acts quietly; a real comparative fork produces a temporary visible object;
the person edits or annotates it directly; the agent continues with that change; the end leaves only a portable work,
decision and receipt. Interface may appear for the matter and fold away when it ends.

This rhythm follows work more closely than a permanent app. It neither keeps every capability visible nor hides every
process forever. Design brings forth the fitting form at the fitting time and converges when the form no longer helps.
** Argument and Debate Scenarios
The two camps need concrete work, or both “natural language can do everything” and “visualization is always fastest”
become slogans. Visualization is often fastest for parallel relations and common work, language for purpose and
exception, and agents for repeated execution. Each kind of speed has places where it does not fit.

#+NAME: triadic-interaction-debate-en
#+EXPLANATION: The scenes presume no winning camp; they test how the three reduce burden while preserving thought.
| Scenario | Reason supporting the judgment | Opposing reason | Evidence that would change the judgment |
|----------+--------------------------------+-----------------+-----------------------------------------|
| One-sentence flight purchase | The purpose is clear and the agent can absorb most operation | Total invisibility hides time, price and change tradeoffs | Most ordinary trips need no app browsing, while one decisive comparison lets people understand exceptions |
| Coding with Codex | Dialogue directs, agent acts, and diffs and tests make the work inspectable | Expert developers sometimes edit faster and dialogue can break flow | People move freely among code, results and dialogue, with errors found earlier |
| AI-assisted visual design | Agent produces alternatives quickly; canvas supports feeling and shaping | Too many candidates crowd out aesthetic formation and model styles converge | People leave their own trace, reduce candidates and reshape structure rather than choose finished outputs |
| Family calendar | Language expresses relations, agent coordinates and a visual timeline reveals burden | A timeline may flatten emotional commitments into colored blocks | Participants can both tell reasons and see temporal effects together before changing the arrangement |
| Accessibility | Speech and agents can bypass unusable GUI | “Visualization is fastest” excludes people who cannot see or do not primarily use vision | The same shared object is equivalently available through visual, speech, touch and structured control |

If pure dialogue eventually provides stable parallel perception, precise manipulation and shared reference in creative
and complex judgment without extra memory burden, this account should change. Conversely, a visual workbench that
keeps people managing tools without improving understanding should not survive merely because it looks professional.
** Principle and Governance Boundary
This document proposes only the Reflection judgment that interaction forms should compose around cognitive and
practical need. It prescribes neither an amount of visualization for every product nor a Charter Principle called “the
triad.” A future principle needs a separate scope, accessibility obligations, counterexamples and failure modes.

Governance decides who may revise standards with which evidence from life and work, how visual designers or model
providers are prevented from monopolizing definition, and how commercial pressure to make interaction needlessly
larger or smaller is handled. Content judgment and admission governance remain separate.

* Counterexamples
First, “visualization is always fastest” is too absolute. Vision overloads and excludes blind people; linear language
can be more natural for story, motive and ambiguity. What remains is a shared object that can be perceived and directly
manipulated in several ways, not visual supremacy.

Second, natural language alone can be lighter than generating a temporary interface. Drawing a complex panel for one
simple confirmation rebuilds the old app.

Third, the triadic relation can become an excuse to make every product more complex. If an action is clearly authorized,
reversible and has no material fork, quiet agent completion and a short receipt are enough.

Fourth, a person may want direct immersion in a tool without agent intervention. The new system must let people bypass
the agent; otherwise “collaboration” becomes another mandatory intermediary.
* Provisional Conclusion
AI does not simply end interface. It ends the premise that every capability must first become a page operated by a
person. Clear-purpose errands such as booking can be carried mainly through language and agents. Code, design and
complex collaboration still need a work space that can be perceived, changed and jointly referenced.

The relation among human, agent and design organizes interaction around how a matter is understood, completed and
reconsidered rather than around an app. What should diminish is obedience to software structure. What should grow is
perception of real relations, differences in the work and consequences in life. A fitting aesthetic is neither always
empty nor always dense: let what matters appear fully and let what does not matter converge naturally.
* Questions That Remain
Games, music, architecture, medical imaging, data analysis, repair and care still need to test this account. Which
shared objects cannot be replaced by language? Which visual conventions are merely occupational habit? Will generated
interfaces create instability and learning difficulty? How can temporary interfaces retain accessibility, consistency
and trustworthy state?

Aesthetic power also remains: who decides what deserves fuller view, who names their own minimal style “room,” and who
names density “professional”? If an agent generates a different interface for everyone, will people retain public
forms they can discuss and contest together?
