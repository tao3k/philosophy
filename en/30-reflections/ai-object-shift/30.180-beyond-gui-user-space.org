* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.30.180-beyond-gui-user-space-en
:END:
When one natural-language request can make AI search flights, coordinate a calendar, call for a restaurant reservation
and complete work across applications, must a person still learn and operate every app page by page? When a model
absorbs the old operating process, what should interaction remove from view, and what must it leave with the person?
* Argument
The change brought by large models is not a chat box added to an existing interface. A person's purpose once had to be
translated into menus, fields, filters and a sequence of clicks. The person first learned which app to open, then how
that app classified the world, and finally expressed life in its prescribed order. Now an ordinary sentence can state
the purpose first, while a model crosses those operating layers. Interaction begins to move from people adapting to
software toward software understanding an entrustment.

Apps may therefore retreat sharply as people's primary operating entrance. Airlines, restaurants, calendars, payment
services and maps do not vanish. They may become services and fulfillment capabilities used by a model. What can
vanish is the burden of repeatedly searching, switching, filling forms, reconciling formats and learning every
software convention to complete an ordinary matter. This interaction cannot be imagined only by adding to or
subtracting from traditional GUI.

Yet “done in one sentence” must not mean “say one thing and never see the rest.” A sentence often expresses a wish
without exhausting budget, companions, timing flexibility, refund risk and unacceptable conditions. A model can help
complete context, but it must not quietly turn guesses into decisions. The central question is no longer who clicks
each step, but which difficulties the model may carry and which forks still belong to a person's life.

** Traditional Object and Applicability Boundary

Traditional interface standards mainly address an object in which a person directly operates an interface and
software responds. Their experience of clarity, consistency, recovery, progressive disclosure and accessibility
remains important but serves as a historical baseline. It can constrain interfaces people still operate directly; it
does not by itself answer who authorizes a model acting across systems, who is affected or what proves completion.

Apple or W3C guidance has not become false. Its object is bounded. Extending it without judgment into agentic AI
mistakes “how to make a control usable” for “why may the model act.” It also keeps looking only at the person before a
screen and misses other people affected by messages, calls, calendar changes and shared money.

** Object Shift

The object has shifted from a person alone to an acting relation formed by a person and AI. At some service entrances,
the direct object is even an agent, while the person appears at purpose, boundaries and material forks. There are also
people who did not initiate the task but bear its effects. Design no longer faces one “user” alone; it faces capabilities,
entrustment, affected parties and actions that can change the world.

The move from app-first to purpose-first is the most immediate expression of this change.

Old software presents its own structure first: flights, hotels, orders and membership. People divide life and feed
each part into these entrances. Purpose-first interaction can begin with, “I want to visit my mother next week, return
Sunday evening and not make the trip exhausting.” The system then relates transport, lodging, work calendar and
family commitments instead of asking which of four apps should open first.

#+EXPLANATION: The change is not fewer pages alone; it rearranges a person's words, software capabilities and the location of decisions.
| Old starting point | New possible starting point | What the model may carry | Space that remains with the person |
|--------------------+-----------------------------+--------------------------+------------------------------------|
| Find an app and feature first | State the matter taking place in life | Find services, translate formats and coordinate systems | Restate the purpose without adopting platform categories |
| Fill each filter | Describe preferences and concerns in ordinary language | Complete candidate conditions from context and ask about critical gaps | Leave some preferences unfixed or change them for this occasion |
| Compare across pages | Gather a few genuinely comparable options | Deduplicate, calculate time and cost, mark conflicts | Choose the criteria, choose nothing or decide later |
| Click every step | Complete a sequence of low-risk operations | Search, query, hold, fill and synchronize | Confirm, revise, stop or take over before serious effects |
| Find results across orders and notices | Explain progress around one entrustment | Track fulfillment, detect failure and organize evidence | Understand what happened and be able to question or reverse it |

This is not every service folded into a super-app. If the new entrance still asks people to learn one total menu and
search infinite functions, it magnifies the old structure. Real change lets a concrete purpose appear first, composes
capabilities behind it as needed and lets the relationship converge when the work is done rather than enlarging one
booking into permanent life management.

** Judgment

The present judgment is that a model may become the direct operating object of a service, but it must not become the
sole interpreter and authorizer of consequences in life. Traditional GUI may cease to be a person's daily entrance.
Human space must move from completing every step to deciding purpose, understanding material forks, revising an
entrustment and returning when needed.

Room therefore moves from visual blankness into life.

As operating interfaces diminish, room is no longer mainly what is absent from a screen. It first means that a person
need not spend attention on software's internal organization, complete a permanent personality profile to receive
help, turn every vague wish into an executable goal at once, or accept the life order a model infers merely because it
has found an answer.

Natural language is not automatically humane. A model can follow one sentence with endless questions and turn
conversation into a longer form. It can narrow a new wish too early through old preference, cover uncertainty with
fluent language, or continually expand authority in the name of convenience. Room is therefore not decided by the
presence of GUI. It is decided by whether a person can remain ambiguous, pause, change their mind, choose nothing and
ask a question software did not prepare.

** Hide the operating machinery, not how life will change

A person need not watch every button a model clicks or understand reservation systems, airline interfaces and calendar
protocols. Spreading the whole process back before the person recreates the burden. What should appear are differences
in life: total cost, departure time, recipients of messages, arrangements that change, refundability, where the model
is guessing and who handles failure.

#+NAME: beyond-gui-visibility-en
#+EXPLANATION: New interaction can absorb meaningless operating burden while keeping changes to life, property and relationships understandable.
| Complexity that can be quietly absorbed | What should become visible at the right time | Why |
|------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------+-----|
| Page navigation, field mapping and authenticated lookup | The purpose underway and its decisive conditions | Prevent formally correct work that is wrong in life |
| Multi-platform search, format conversion and deduplication | Price, time, main tradeoffs and reasons options were excluded | A person needs to understand what is being chosen |
| Reversible holds, drafts and read-only queries | Who is affected and whose agreement is missing | One person's convenience cannot decide for shared life |
| Underlying tool calls and ordinary retries | Payment, sending, cancellation or publication about to occur | Material external effects need fitting authority |
| Internal log detail | Completion evidence, failure point, remedy and reversal | “Done” must remain verifiable and answerable |

“Visible” need not always mean a traditional screen. It may be a concise restatement, spoken confirmation, portable
itinerary, haptic notice, durable receipt or record expanded only when needed. Accessibility and setting call for
different forms. What matters is not defending GUI but defending a person's understanding of what will happen.

** Intent inference is not authorization

Work such as UI-JEPA shows that a model can infer likely intent from a sequence of interface actions. Models such as
Muse Spark show that scripts, tools, clicks and cross-application action can be chosen by the model. The [[file:../../40-sources/40.180-ui-intent-and-computer-use.org][source note]]
records these capabilities and boundaries. They make interaction more natural while introducing a distinction that
must hold: understanding that someone probably wants to visit Shanghai does not authorize purchasing a particular
ticket; remembering a past preference for a window seat does not override a companion's need this time.

Understanding is a judgment about meaning. Authorization is an entrustment concerning effects. The two may approach
each other through durable, explicit and revocable arrangements, but better guessing does not make them identical.

** Interaction should vary with consequence, not ask everywhere

If every step requests confirmation, a person moves from clicking through an app to clicking “approve.” If nothing
asks, convenience can become loss of control. New interaction has to converge differently for different effects.

#+NAME: beyond-gui-action-boundaries-en
#+EXPLANATION: Interaction density should follow consequence, reversibility, affected people and existing entrustment, not model capability alone.
| Action setting | How it may proceed | Boundary to preserve |
|----------------+--------------------+----------------------|
| Read-only query, weather or public opening hours | Complete directly and give a brief answer | Source and time remain available on request |
| Search flights, check availability or coordinate a personal calendar draft | Continue within stated scope | Return conflicts, assumptions and a few candidates |
| Reversible reservation or a repeated matter already entrusted | Act under the arrangement and report promptly | Easy cancellation; ask again on exceptional conditions |
| Payment, ticket purchase or external message | State exact object, amount, content and way back | Confirm this instance, or use visible revocable preauthorization |
| Effects on relatives, colleagues or shared money | Identify each party's rights and missing agreement first | One initiator's sentence cannot replace other voices |
| Irreversible or high-consequence action | Stop before the decision and explain alternatives and risk | The appropriate person decides and evidence remains |

This is not an eternal risk table. A restaurant reservation may be easy to cancel for an ordinary gathering and
consequential for a wedding or allergy accommodation. Moving one calendar event may place extra labor on a caregiver.
The model must understand context, and the product must let people change default boundaries rather than making
intelligence an excuse for overreach.

** Argument and Debate Scenarios

The topic cannot merely list a set of design requirements. Its judgment must face support and opposition in concrete
change. These scenes deliberately retain dispute: the same automation may release attention while hiding price,
transferring labor or crossing another person's boundary.

| Agent adjusts a calendar | Finds feasible time among many constraints quickly | One person's efficiency may transfer care and waiting to others | Every affected party's authority and burden enters the judgment, not only the initiator's optimization |
| Agent transacts with agent | Reduces human formatting and operating cost | Models may form commitments, discriminate in price or mislead each other without human understanding | Commitments return to authorized people, rules are auditable and disputes have a reachable human exit |

*** What one-sentence flight booking actually means

“Book a flight to Chengdu next week” can begin searching, checking the calendar, using preferences already permitted
and finding a few candidates. If date, budget or companions remain unclear, the model asks only questions that change
the outcome instead of rebuilding the old form in conversation. Before payment it states flight, time, total price,
baggage, change terms and companion status. Afterward it gives portable evidence and continues the already agreed work
needed for delay, failure or cancellation.

Real convenience is neither dozens of app steps compressed into an invisible gamble nor every page replaced by a
dialogue turn. The model carries the labor of finding and coordinating, while the person returns only where life truly
forks.

Calling for a table works similarly. A person may state time, party size and important needs while the model handles
busy lines, accents, repeated confirmation and calendar entry. It must not disclose allergy, birthday or relationship
information from an old record without cause, or replace no availability with an arrangement that embarrasses the
party. Natural language makes entrustment resemble human speech and therefore demands respect for what speech leaves
unsaid.

*** From one transaction to an understandable entrustment

Traditional apps often define completion as a page becoming “successful.” An agentic task may span hours or days:
waiting for a call back, watching fares, coordinating others and handling a schedule change. A person should not guard
the process, but neither should they discover at the end that the system made a chain of decisions for them.

A fitting relationship resembles an entrustment. State purpose and boundaries at the beginning; return during work
only for a real fork, risk or failure; explain what was done, what failed and what responsibility remains at the end.
Long-lived entrustment must also be inspectable, revisable and withdrawable. One old sentence must not extend forever.

** Principle and Governance Boundary

This document offers a Reflection judgment still exposed to contradiction by scenarios. It does not thereby become a
normative Principle. A future claim such as “material effects require decision by the appropriate person” must be
written separately in the Charter with scope, counterargument, failure modes and sources; Governance then decides
admission and revision. The topic contract only prevents reflection from skipping object shift, judgment and debate.
It neither supplies principle content nor replaces governance procedure.

* Counterexamples
First, natural language is not always fastest. Professionals may need dense tables, maps, timelines and direct
manipulation. Some choices require several relations to remain visible at once, while pure dialogue makes comparison
difficult. A new architecture should bring forth fitting visual and manipulable forms rather than making chat the only
entrance.

Second, a model can mishear, misremember or follow hostile instructions embedded in a page. The more systems it can
cross, the farther one error travels. One-sentence completion enlarges invisible risk unless authority, source and
affected objects have clear boundaries.

Third, constant confirmation trains mechanical approval and formally hands responsibility back to the user. Full
automation can overreach. Risk tiers that miss lived context can also treat a low-price but emotionally serious matter
as trivial.

Fourth, not everyone wants to disclose context in natural language, and models do not understand every language,
accent and expressive habit equally. Direct operation, human service and accessible alternatives must remain.

Fifth, service providers may change price, ranking and visibility for agent entrances. Comparison by a model does not
automatically make markets fair. A platform's commercial preference may become advice a person mistakes for neutral.
* Provisional Conclusion
The interaction change brought by AI does not merely make traditional apps thinner. It can remove the app from its
necessary position between a person and a purpose in life. A model can take up search, switching, forms, waiting and
coordination so ordinary speech connects directly to complex capabilities. Often, the person truly need not open an
app.

The departure of the operating interface must not take the person's living space with it. New room means not learning
the machine's internal structure or continually watching it. New convergence means returning only the forks that
genuinely belong to the person. We need not preserve every click. We do need places where purpose can be restated,
material effects understood, companions speak, entrustment withdrawn and failure questioned.

Good future interaction neither trains people to become more proficient users of AI nor lets AI arrange everything
out of sight. It moves complex operation farther from the person while keeping the decisions that matter in life near.
* Questions That Remain
We still need to hear blind people, people who cannot or prefer not to speak, people with changing cognition, people in
places with sparse digital services, professionals who depend on dense interfaces, service workers and those receiving
agent-made calls. Automation convenient to one person may transfer verification, waiting and emotional labor to the
other end.

We do not yet know how lasting entrustment can learn context without sealing identity; how a model negotiates for a
person without impersonating them; how human commitments remain real when several agents book, reschedule and bargain;
how natural-language entrances avoid becoming a new platform monopoly; or how ordinary people discover public rules,
price differences and appeal paths after traditional apps recede.
