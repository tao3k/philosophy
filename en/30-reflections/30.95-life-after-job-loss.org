* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.life-after-job-loss-en
:END:
Zhisheng supports a person experiencing change, carrying responsibilities, and retaining aspirations, not someone waiting for an algorithm to arrange life. A newly unemployed parent asks: “School and rent still need paying. Should I take any job quickly, seek a suitable one, or try changing careers?”

Employment, care, relocation, marriage, parenthood, and retirement are not a mandatory sequence. Start from the person's relationships and ends: which conditions does their chosen life need, how can those conditions be built, and which paths remain after disruption? [[file:30.91-financial-life-simulation.org][Zhisheng]] should reveal possibilities, not merely prevent mistakes.
* Argument
Job loss can be an observed event. Permanent unemployment or a predetermined future for a child does not follow from it. Neither present disasters as destiny nor guarantee recovery. Separate actual changes, untested fears, necessary responsibilities, and adjustable paths.

** One household, three revisable paths
This is an entirely fictional teaching case, not a household budget standard. Available cash is CNY 60,000; the other parent earns 6,000 net monthly; essentials cost 10,000 monthly, including housing, living costs, and existing care. The household hopes to preserve a 20,000 buffer. The baseline monthly gap is 4,000. The common stress assumption is no new long-term job for six months, not a forecast of unemployment duration.

#+NAME: job-loss-paths-en
| Path | Additional six-month assumptions | Terminal cash, no shock | With one 10,000 expense | Unresolved question |
|------+----------------------------------+-------------------------+-------------------------+---------------------|
| A: seek work in the original field | No extra income or costs | 36,000 | 26,000 | Vacancies, search progress, waiting costs |
| B: retrain for a career change | 6,000 course fee in month 1; 1,000 extra care in each of the first three months | 27,000 | 17,000 | Course relevance to jobs and available time |
| C: temporary work alongside searching | 3,000 extra net income and 1,000 extra care monthly from month 1 | 48,000 | 38,000 | Work availability and conflicts with searching or family time |

Temporary net income deducts direct work costs but excludes the care cost listed separately. The unexpected expense happens once, at the end of any month 1–6. Each path has net outflow every month here, making the terminal balance the minimum; this does not generalize to paths that lose money before earning it. Intramonth solvency, long-term employment, and remaining care capacity are unproven.

C's larger cash balance does not make it the best life; B's smaller balance does not invalidate a career aspiration. B breaches the household target under stress, so reconsider the implementation: a lower-cost trial, staged payments, or different care arrangements, subject to their actual availability, rather than abandon the whole career direction.

** Job loss has happened; training remains an option
Job loss changes present circumstances. Considering training means neither enrollment nor inevitable future employment. Situating both together lets us ask what conditions are still missing between the present and a possible path.

#+CAPTION: Job loss belongs to the present event cut; training remains an uncommitted counterfactual. Positions zero and one denote logical order, not months.

** Which plans need another look?
Housing and training may depend on the same reserve; temporary work may take time previously available for searching or care. After income changes, locate related plans and inspect how effects travel through those relationships.

#+NAME: job-loss-dependencies-en
#+CAPTION: Find plans depending on the household's shared cash.
#+begin_src gql :eval never :exports code
MATCH (plan:Plan)-[:DEPENDS_ON]->(resource:Resource)
WHERE resource.name = 'shared-cash'
RETURN plan
#+end_src

#+NAME: job-loss-impact-en
#+CAPTION: x depends on y; if y changes or is affected, include x among the items requiring inspection.
#+begin_src ascent :eval never :exports code
relation depends(&'static str, &'static str);
relation changed(&'static str);
relation affected(&'static str);

affected(x) <-- changed(x);
affected(x) <-- depends(x, y), affected(y);
#+end_src

Given teaching facts that shared cash depends on salary and housing and training plans depend on shared cash, a salary change puts those plans in the affected set. =affected= means inspect, not failed, and establishes no causal magnitude. A relation between temporary-work hours and care must likewise be declared and checked first.

Each judgment should preserve reasons and fact versions: which changes support it, and which new information invalidates it. Writing a rule that training causes higher income cannot turn an untested relationship into fact.

An event is what happened; an Effect is a resulting change, marked expected or hypothetical before occurrence; Impact identifies affected resources, goals, responsibilities, and people; a trajectory unfolds these through time. Alternative universes are only a metaphor: branches do not exhaust the future. A new job requires fresh observation and inference, not an optimistic annotation beside stale conclusions.

** The same money can support a commitment only once
One reserve cannot fully underwrite several plans simultaneously, and one evening cannot simultaneously provide work, learning, and care. Cash buffers concern the whole path, not just its endpoint.

#+NAME: job-loss-equations-en
#+CAPTION: C is cash, I income, E essentials, F one-off costs, K extra care, and S shocks; no category is counted twice.
#+begin_src typst :eval never :exports code
$ C_(t+1) = C_t + I_t - E_t - F_t - K_t - S_t $
$ C_6^A = 60000 + 6 (6000 - 10000) = 36000 $
$ C_6^B = C_6^A - 6000 - 3 times 1000 = 27000 $
$ C_6^C = C_6^A + 6 (3000 - 1000) = 48000 $
$ C_6^(B, "shock") = 27000 - 10000 = 17000 < 20000 $
$ "buffer holds" equiv min_(0 <= t <= 6) C_t >= 20000 $
#+end_src

These equations measure neither career meaning, a child's interests, nor care quality. Correct amounts do not establish acceptable demands on family time or participation.

** The next shock is unknown; the next commitment can still be constrained
Some risks have unknown timing or scale; other changes are not yet represented. We cannot guarantee their absence, but can require that invalidated grounds no longer authorize new commitments. Renewed understanding is a condition for continuing action.

The earlier hope to preserve 20,000 is a discussable preference, not a payment prohibition the system may harden on its own. The logical excerpt below adds an explicit teaching assumption: after seeing which payments the threshold would block, how long it applies and when it will be reviewed, someone with decision standing confirms it as a local boundary that AI must not cross without renewed authorization. If the household retains it only as a soft target, =authorized= cannot arise from it; the system may explain consequences and request a decision.

#+NAME: job-loss-risk-en
#+CAPTION: Under the teaching assumption of an explicitly confirmed 20,000 local payment boundary, this 6,000 payment still needs a current basis and authorization; shocks or pending review withdraw the old authorization.
#+begin_src tla :eval never :exports code
vars == <<cash, current, review, authorized>>
Permit == current /\ ~review /\ authorized /\ cash - 6000 >= 20000
Pay == /\ Permit /\ cash' = cash - 6000 /\ authorized' = FALSE
       /\ UNCHANGED <<current, review>>
Shock(s) == /\ s >= 0 /\ cash' = cash - s
            /\ current' = FALSE /\ review' = TRUE /\ authorized' = FALSE
PauseUnknown == /\ current' = FALSE /\ review' = TRUE /\ authorized' = FALSE
                /\ UNCHANGED cash
StaleCannotAuthorize == (~current \/ review) => ~authorized
#+end_src

Passing =Permit= establishes only that this 6,000 payment satisfies the current local admission condition. It does not establish that the 4,000 monthly gap will preserve the buffer, or that training, care and employment form a sustainable path. Intertemporal affordability requires the cash trajectory, grounds for income recovery, exit terms and other obligations. Real recovery also depends on adjusting work, obtaining support and rebuilding conditions. Narrow correctness must not sponsor a broader judgment about living.

When an unquantifiable change is discovered, leaving the recorded amount unchanged does not assign it zero impact: the old figure no longer supports new payments. Resumption needs new facts, affected people's participation, and explicit authorization. Review cannot guarantee eventual employment either.

Environmental changes are not controlled by AI, so never breaching the buffer is no unconditional invariant. A counterexample adds a 10,000 expense to the baseline 4,000 gap in each of three successive months: cash moves 60,000 → 46,000 → 32,000 → 18,000, even without tuition. This is a separate, stronger scenario than the table's single shock, not a silent change of assumptions.

The aim is to avoid new controllable commitments on an invalid basis, expose gaps, and stop unauthorized actions, not guarantee that nature cannot harm people. A bounded risk model cannot replace continued understanding of changed circumstances.

** Three months later: what must the same household understand again?
Continue the same fictional household without treating the story as actual evidence. Suppose four observations are stipulated after three months: temporary work is real but its evening shift conflicts with school pickup; a low-cost trial reveals that the unemployed parent does not want that industry but values control over work time more clearly; the other parent refuses additional evening care without disclosing private reasons; and cash is better than the scenario expected while every household member reports greater exhaustion.

#+NAME: job-loss-three-month-review-en
| New observation | Why prior advice changes | A possible next step now |
|-----------------+--------------------------+--------------------------|
| A temporary job exists, but its hours conflict | Existence did not convert into a feasible opportunity for this household | Check other shifts or pickup support, or reject the job; do not book income early |
| A trial changes the career purpose | The data need not be wrong; experience made the purpose more specific | Stop this course expense, retain what was learned, and explore other autonomous work structures |
| A relative refuses new care | The assumed relational condition was never authorized | Do not demand irrelevant private reasons; rearrange time without construing refusal as rejection of the family |
| Cash is better than expected, but everyone is more exhausted | A monetary improvement did not establish a better life | Reduce concurrent tasks and observe rest and care rather than silencing people with the balance |

The review exposes four different issues: actual availability, changed purpose, authority and relationship conditions, and evaluative framing. They should not all become “prediction error.” Conversely, if the person understands the conflicts and still chooses a meaningful commitment that crosses a soft target without crossing an explicit authorization boundary, the system should help define scope, exit or remedy rather than repeatedly impose the lowest-risk path.

** A child's future is not a household return on investment
If a child wants music lessons during an income interruption, neither automatically cancel them nor demand unaffordable spending for future returns. Invite age-appropriate expression of interests, check possible equipment loans or staged learning, and distinguish temporary adjustment from a long-term change needing discussion. A budget constrains one means of realization, not automatically the aspiration or right to participate. The other parent's care is not an unlimited free variable.

Preparing before rain means bounded responses; considering disruption means understanding dependencies; aligning commitments with resources means neither fear, extreme austerity, nor blame. [[https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/educator-tools/financial-well-being-resources/explore-findings/][CFPB's discussion]] likewise recognizes factors within and beyond individual control. When resources are insufficient, disclose gaps honestly, verify support, and preserve dignity.

* Counterexamples
Temporary work can crowd out better job opportunities; cheap training can still waste time. A family may reasonably prefer a meaningful career investment. An undiscovered dependency is not an absent one, and no alert does not mean no risk. Formal languages expose inconsistencies but cannot supply missing values, real opportunities, or other people's consent.

Staged review can also demand endless self-explanation, postpone commitment or chase every temporary emotion. Purpose revision requires renewed endorsement by the person, not system cancellation after short-term fluctuation. A relative's specific refusal must not expand automatically into rejection of every shared responsibility.
* Provisional Conclusion
An event changes circumstances without determining a whole life. Zhisheng should turn aspirations into conditions that can be built gradually, help people understand requirements, sacrifices, and reasons to change direction, and reduce burdens within authority. Ecosystem tools connect by serving this question, not by turning every logical excerpt into a running system.
* Promotion Criteria
Challenge the argument across family structures, care conditions, income levels, and goals beyond employment. Use real feedback to test whether advice increases agency rather than anxiety. Future execution or formal-verification claims need versions, data, complete models, and receipts; Org code blocks do not themselves establish implementation closure.

Also test that converting a soft preference into an execution boundary involves understanding and confirmation, that local admission is never presented as long-term sustainability, and that longitudinal feedback distinguishes factual updates, changed purposes, relational refusal and evaluative bias.
