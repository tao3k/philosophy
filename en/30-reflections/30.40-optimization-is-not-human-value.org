* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: reflection-optimization-is-not-human-value-en
:END:

Why can improvement of an objective function not be directly interpreted as human welfare, consent, or realized value?

[[file:../40-sources/40.20-gu-yanwu-practical-learning.org][source note]] · [[file:../10-charter/10.30-human-agency.org][target thematic Charter]]

* Argument

An objective selects measurable proxies and necessarily omits context, rights, distribution, fairness, and future option value. Human agents must be able to define, refuse, override, and challenge goals.

* Counterexamples

Some closed control problems safely optimize one metric; users can explicitly delegate preferences; excessive intervention can damage usability.

* Provisional Conclusion

Optimization ranks candidate actions; it cannot independently create normative authority or human consent.

* Promotion Criteria

Promote when consent, override, contestability, and impact receipts become engineering requirements.
