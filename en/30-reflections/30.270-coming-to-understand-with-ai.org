* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.30.270-coming-to-understand-with-ai-en
:END:
[[file:30.220-making-what-matters-possible.org][The person photographing the morning street]] opens his pictures again. “I've taken so many, but they still aren't what I wanted.” He has not asked to become a professional photographer or said he only wants a finished album. Natural language lets him begin with an unfinished thought. How will the explanations AI offers help shape his understanding of himself and his work?

It can move from composition to urban memory, from sequence to accounts of labor, bringing knowledge he has not encountered. But if he cannot distinguish these directions or see how to try them, those possibilities are not yet his choices. The question is not how much more a model can generate. When should help open the unknown, when should it make differences clearer, and when should it settle down to doing what has been decided? This continues an imagined person's story, not an interview record.
* Argument
Unequal knowledge can make learning and delegation possible without becoming a reason for obedience. We propose that help should open or narrow with the activity, while making key differences accessible, offering appropriate grounds, and leaving room to change direction. Possibility, the burden of understanding, and reliable action all matter to this account.

** Knowing more does not settle each person's place
Someone need not acquire a helper's breadth of knowledge to be entitled to ask for help. Yet breadth, reliable judgment about a particular problem, and understanding someone's life are different things. Offering many explanations does not establish which applies here. Describing a choice's consequences does not grant authority to decide which consequences are worth bearing.

Human experience deserves attention, not a declaration of infallibility. “These photographs disappoint me” is a feeling to hear; “therefore I have no eye for things” is a further judgment. AI can help distinguish them and offer reasons the person had not considered. The division is not simply human values and machine facts: both sides interpret and may overlook something.

The difficulty is that I can learn from you without being able to audit everything you say. What we seek is not equal knowledge but conditions for participation: access to the reasons that matter, room to point out what does not fit, other grounds to consult, and bounded delegation without understanding every underlying principle.

** Ask what kind of help is needed now
The same photographer may want very different help at different moments. Before deciding what the album is about, a new direction can matter. When several sequences look different but he cannot say why, comparison may help. Once the content is settled and he wants two copies for his family, accurate page order, specifications, and delivery information matter instead.

| Present need | What AI can offer | When to reconsider the approach |
|--------------+-------------------+---------------------------------|
| Explore possibilities not yet imagined | Unfamiliar perspectives, examples, and reasoned conjectures | The person is choosing labels without seeing their differences or relevance |
| Understand differences already encountered | Compare concrete material, explain key reasons, try something small together | Repeated explanations do not help; change the medium or return to the question |
| Complete what has been decided | Check conditions, act within the agreed scope, report actual results | A new fact could change the choice; reopen discussion rather than proceed blindly |

“I'd like to look around” can invite exploration, while “let's finish this first” should genuinely narrow the help. AI need not ask for a mode on every turn. Where uncertain, it can offer a light choice and let the person change their mind. Past clicks should not permanently identify someone as a beginner, an explorer, or a person who only wants delegation.

Needing certainty does not mean needing a confident tone. If printing specifications remain unchecked, establish them or explain what cannot be guaranteed. Repeating the same answer does not improve its grounds. Conversely, an unverified idea can remain useful in exploration, provided it does not quietly become a fact when action begins.

** How borrowed words become one's own understanding
If AI immediately says “you lack visual storytelling,” he may learn many terms while prematurely treating his dissatisfaction as technical inadequacy. Perhaps he wants to preserve an encounter with the shopkeeper. Perhaps he dislikes the added captions. Perhaps none of these explanations fits. Three options are no remedy if all rest on the same unquestioned assumption.

Two photographs can lie side by side: one draws attention to the person opening the shop, the other to the wait before its doors open. AI can describe what it notices, ask whether those differences matter to him, and allow “neither.” He can point, recount a memory, or move a photograph without first delivering a complete explanation of himself. Natural language is an opening, not the only qualification for expression.

If he then asks why changing the order changes the story, knowledge about narrative has found a place to enter. AI can explain further or arrange the same photographs in two ways so that a new concept illuminates an actual difference. Understanding deepens not when ordinary words become professional vocabulary, but when previously indistinct relationships become visible.

Nor must help always start small. An unexpected finished work or a complex diagram may first show someone what they want to ask. The question is whether they can find a way into it, not whether small always precedes large. Difficulty now may reflect fatigue, language, or missing explanation; it should not become a permanent assessment of someone's intellectual limits.

** Making possibilities usable
AI can compare more directions in the background without displaying every intermediate attempt at once. But organizing possibilities brings responsibility: why were these paths retained? Showing only what the model considers most suitable may settle a person's choices before they see them.

One approach is to offer a few genuinely different directions, explain what each attends to, and leave room to explore further, introduce another path, or revisit what was omitted. Abundance need not mean a heap of material, and simplicity need not conceal selection. A person's limited attention deserves care; their curiosity may also exceed the territory a system has marked out for them.

Exploration may matter through a new question or a previously unknown way to try, not only through a final selection. Moving from “how can I take better photographs?” to “what sort of day do I want my family to see?” may matter more than ten styles. Whether it does must be learned from his experience, not announced by a model as a personal breakthrough.

** Letting an explanation meet the world
Feeling understood by an account does not establish its explanation of causes. [[file:../40-sources/40.270-understanding-and-reliance.org][The source note on understanding and reliance]] helps frame this distinction without judging the photographer. For this album, he could remove captions or change the order of one group of pictures and notice what changes for him. A trial can open a question without one preference settling all its causes.

#+EXPLANATION: A sentence may contain several kinds of judgment; examine the particular claim rather than assign one credibility score to a person or conversation.
| What is being claimed? | What grounds might help? | What cannot substitute? |
|------------------------+--------------------------+-------------------------|
| A fact | Original material, dates, and checkable records | Fluent narration does not replace checking |
| A cause | Other explanations, counterexamples, and suitable small trials | Sequence alone does not establish causation |
| An account of personal experience | Particular experiences, corrections, and revisiting across circumstances | Immediate resonance does not establish a personality |
| What is worth doing | Reasons, costs, relationships, and trade-offs someone is willing to bear | A score or majority view cannot settle value alone |

Verification cannot circulate only among a model's explanation, its chosen material, and the objections it simulates. Return to the photographs, check where a description came from, and, if wanted, hear someone who knows the street. Another model may help, but agreement based on the same material and assumptions is not independent evidence. A real person's response can also be mistaken; it calls for understanding, not automatic deference.

When new experience does not fit an explanation, revision must be possible instead of adding stories to protect it. Sometimes a reasonable result is “this explains part of it; I still don't know about the rest.” Greater clarity about what cannot be asserted can itself be progress. Repeatable execution, traceable sources, and personal endorsement each matter, but none alone guarantees truth.

** I can rely on you without first becoming you
Understanding key reasons should not become an examination required before receiving help. The photographer may want to learn sequencing or simply have the album printed. Reliable completion with a tool is valuable in itself. If we additionally claim that his judgment has grown, that change needs separate inquiry; the finished work's quality cannot supply the evidence.

A helper should do the checking and explaining it can, rather than hand the user an enormous reading list, every possible error, and all responsibility at once. Higher-stakes matters call for stronger grounds and, where necessary, professional help with relevant competence and responsibility. Reading an explanation several times cannot make an ordinary person solely responsible. Trusted supporters, visual explanations, and accessibility assistance can also help someone understand key conditions.

Continual doubt can exhaust people, too. [[file:../40-sources/40.270-understanding-and-reliance.org][The related research]] gives reason to question whether more thinking burden necessarily means better help. What deserves pursuit is grounded, revisable trust: neither starting from suspicion on every occasion nor never asking again after one satisfying result.

** Understanding can remain without defining a person forever
If this conversation is worth retaining, [[file:30.110-co-maintained-understanding.org][co-maintained understanding]] needs to distinguish what he said, what AI proposed, what was actually compared, and what remains unknown. It might remember that he kept the waiting scene in this sequence, rather than infer that he essentially prefers waiting and solitude. Changed circumstances must leave old interpretations open to revision, reduced importance, or disuse.

This is a semantic requirement proposed for TAO3K's personal knowledge graph, not a claim of an implemented feature: statements of different origins must not collapse into one kind of fact, nor conjectures become knowledge merely through long storage. Concrete responsibilities for POO Flow, MRR, and ASP require separate scrutiny through the [[file:../20-engineering/20.10-cross-repository-realization-map.org][engineering map]]. This essay introduces no new representation language and treats no receipt as proof that a person understands.
* Counterexamples
Someone may want to spend substantial time exploring an unfamiliar field; narrowing too soon would limit curiosity. Someone else needs a small task completed and should not be made to learn because a model can teach more deeply. The same person may choose differently on another day. No single conversational pace should become a universal rule.

“Explain at the person's level” can become paternalism: a system decides they will not understand and withholds complex but important reasons. Conversely, displaying everything may offer openness only in form. We need to learn whether someone can actually go deeper when needed, not merely whether a “learn more” option exists.

Surprise, intuition, and inarticulate feeling do not all admit repeatable tests. A poem may open experience without proving a proposition; a friend's response need not be an experimental result. Claims about facts, causes, or general effects call for corresponding evidence. Difficulty testing an experience should not erase it, and appealing to experience should not exempt every claim from scrutiny.

People may also understand reasons and still judge wrongly. Participation does not guarantee correctness. A low-risk trial cannot provide the same assurance for an irreversible major decision. If help increases confidence while making contrary evidence harder to notice, it substantially challenges this essay's hope for shared understanding.
* Provisional Conclusion
A model's expansive reach becomes help within a situation: opening possibilities in exploration, making important differences visible in understanding, and reliably carrying out what has been decided in execution. These can alternate but cannot stand in for one another. Many ideas do not prove more choice; a complete explanation does not prove deep understanding; completed execution does not prove learning.

Provisionally, we seek a relationship in which someone can draw on broad knowledge without pretending to possess the same breadth, gradually knowing why to trust, how far, and when to reconsider. Good help has room for the unfamiliar and for “that's enough for today.”
* Questions That Remain
Real observation should begin with matters participants choose to bring, without requiring a learning goal. Across languages, experience, energy, and available support, which explanations make differences visible, and which merely make it embarrassing to admit confusion? People must be able to decline follow-up without losing basic help.

A proposed Principle needs its own scope and counterexamples. Who arranges observation, what is retained, and how people leave are Governance questions. This remains a Reflection: completing it does not complete verification of human understanding.
