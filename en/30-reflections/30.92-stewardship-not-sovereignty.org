* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.stewardship-not-sovereignty-en
:END:
When AI is authorized to manage a person's means continuously, how can it avoid gradually taking ownership of the person's ends?

[[file:30.60-action-requires-authority.org][Action and Authority]] · [[file:../40-sources/40.94-financial-planning-support-boundaries.org][planning and support boundary]]
* Argument
Stewardship is entrusted care; sovereignty is final power over ends and exceptions. AI may observe, explain, simulate, propose, and manage within explicit delegation, such as moving a bounded amount into a named reserve. Managing means does not silently reprioritize housing, entrepreneurship, care, or present quality of life.

“Delegated Means, Human Ends” requires principal, purpose, resource, action, amount, duration, risk, review, and revocation. Higher return does not authorize a new asset class, longer commitment, or sacrificed liquidity.

Observe, Explain, Simulate, Propose, Manage, and Act are distinct authority layers. Movement toward irreversible change requires more specific authority and assurance. Crossing a layer is explicit, not natural product drift.
* Counterexamples
People may sincerely delegate broad goals and want less repeated decision-making. Stewardship permits bounded policies and defaults when people can understand and exit and silence cannot expand scope. AI may clarify unstable ends but cannot convert inferred preference into authority.
* Provisional Conclusion
AI's legitimate role is revocable stewardship: remain faithful to human ends, manage means inside authority, expose conflict, and return changes of purpose to the person. Computational advantage does not create sovereignty over a life.
* Promotion Criteria
Show that opportunity cannot expand scope, inferred preference cannot become authority, revocation stops future action, and conflicts of purpose return to a person. Cover emergencies, incapacity, joint accounts, and multiple beneficiaries.
