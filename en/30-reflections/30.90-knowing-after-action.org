* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.knowing-after-action-en
:END:
Why is a successful action return not the end of an epistemic loop? How should outcomes, errors, and unintended consequences re-enter knowledge state so a system genuinely knows again after acting?

[[file:../40-sources/40.80-dewey-action-experience-learning.org][Dewey source note]] · [[file:30.10-knowledge-action-in-agent-age.org][Unity Without Collapse]]
* Argument
Action places a judgment into the world and lets the world answer. Dewey's processual and transactional account of experience suggests that experience is not another record inside a subject. It is the relation of organism and environment through doing, undergoing, adjustment, and readjustment. For an agent, invoking a tool is only doing; receiving and interpreting consequence begins the undergoing.

Execution receipt, outcome, and effect must therefore remain distinct. A receipt may establish that a request was accepted, a command exited, or an API returned. An outcome says whether the intended state arose. Effects include externalities, delayed impact, and changes borne by other subjects. Treating the first as the others makes the system declare completion exactly where learning is most needed.

Error is not an edge case outside the loop but an entrance to knowledge revision. It may show that facts changed, a model does not apply, authority was insufficient, execution diverged, observation was distorted, or the value objective was incomplete. Different errors must update different objects. Recording all of them as “failure” discards the structure needed to learn, while retrying all of them can amplify harm.

Learning requires at least four moves: observe the difference between result and expectation; attribute the difference to assumptions that remain contestable; update a claim, scope, policy, or capability; and reassess earlier decisions that depended on it. Reconciliation is not merely eventual database consistency. It lets past action change future reasons.
* Counterexamples
Not every action is a legitimate experiment. Irreversible, high-risk, or rights-affecting action cannot lower its authority threshold merely because learning has value. Experimental value never automatically overrides consent, dignity, or safety.

World feedback is not a transparent answer. Noise, selection bias, delay, and simultaneous change may contaminate outcomes. Observing B after action A does not establish that A caused B; the learning loop still needs causal and measurement discipline.

Updating too quickly chases noise, while updating too slowly preserves failure. Systems need confidence, observation windows, and replication conditions rather than treating adaptability as immediate principle revision after every result.
* Provisional Conclusion
Action is not the terminus of knowledge but an experimental condition for the next cycle of knowing. A closed loop lets knowledge enter constrained action, action produce observable consequence, consequence undergo reconciliation and causal review, and the result alter revisable knowledge state.

“Success” therefore needs at least two statements: whether execution completed according to protocol and whether the world moved toward the authorized objective. When the latter is unknown, the system should remain unknown or pending rather than borrow the success code of the former.
* Promotion Criteria
Validate the update loop with reversible operations, delayed consequence, partial failure, and unobservable result. Define which differences trigger stopping, remedy, retry, or epistemic downgrade. Show that learning does not turn people who did not consent into experimental subjects. Only then consider deepening the practical-learning Charter rather than adding another abstract slogan.
