* Question
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: philosophy.reflection.traceable-knowledge-en
:END:
A claim with real-world authority should be able to explain both why it was known and what followed from treating it as knowledge. Why is traceability that ends at a file source or model input insufficient for responsibility?

[[file:30.10-knowledge-action-in-agent-age.org][Unity Without Collapse]] · [[file:../40-sources/40.60-peirce-fallibilism-inquiry.org][Peirce source note]]
* Argument
Traceability first has an epistemic direction. A claim should lead back to the inference that produced it; the inference should expose evidence and assumptions; the evidence should lead to observations; and the observations should identify source, time, version, conditions, and missing scope. This chain does not guarantee truth. It lets another person reconstruct why belief was warranted at that time.

#+begin_example
claim ← inference ← evidence ← observation ← source
#+end_example

There is also a practical direction. Once knowledge participates in judgment, it may alter decisions, authority, actions, and effects. Forward traceability does not predict every future. It preserves the choices that actually occurred so the consequence of a claim does not disappear at the execution boundary.

#+begin_example
knowledge → judgment → decision → authority → action → effect → observation
#+end_example

Together the two directions turn provenance into answerability. Maintainers can ask which evidence supported an action, which authority permitted a change to the world, and whether harm calls for revising an observation, inference, scope, decision rule, or authority arrangement. Answerability is not automatic blame. It is first the capacity of a system and organization to answer why.

The trace must also represent absences: excluded sources, unsatisfied premises, rejected proposals, withheld authority, and outcomes not yet observed. Recording only the successful route creates false inevitability and hides the alternatives that were genuinely available.
** Trace presentation as well as inputs
Life judgment needs more than the data behind an answer: why these options, why this ordering, which purposes were expressed and which inferred? An undisplayed direction may never enter comparison. Recording only the final confirmation can portray narrowed choices as adequate choice.

Explanations must be usable by affected people. “Postpone the course because care arrangements remain unconfirmed” should distinguish insufficient facts, value conflict and a conservative system default. People need to correct particular premises, not merely confront incomprehensible model scores.

** Narratives are an entrance to evidence, not noise to clean away
“I completed the plan but feel less like myself” is not an established causal conclusion, but it is a reason to inquire again. Loneliness, exhaustion, respect and creative meaning need not acquire a scale before being heard. Preserve who speaks, context and interpretive boundaries without stripping the account of its meaning.

Conversely, traceability must not force repeated proof of suffering or disclosure of irrelevant private matters. Information sufficient for present review differs from permission to reuse a life story indefinitely. Necessary decision reasons may survive deletion of sensitive detail, but deleted evidence must not be fabricated.

** Records must lead to correction
If advice imposes unnegotiated labor on a relative, a log proving only that the primary user clicked confirmation records an inadequate procedure. Someone must be able to explain, stop future arrangements, discuss remedy and identify dependent plans. Responsibilities in [[file:30.97-shared-lives-and-responsibility.org][shared life]] cannot be washed away by an event chain.

* Counterexamples
Retaining every intermediate state can violate privacy, expose security boundaries, or bury low-risk work under audit cost. Traceability is not unlimited retention; consequence, sensitivity, and reversibility should determine granularity, duration, and access.

A provenance chain may also be formally complete yet epistemically empty: sources copy one another, evidence is irrelevant to the conclusion, observations have expired, or a receipt proves command completion without proving real effect. The existence of a chain cannot replace evaluation of its relations.

Some human experience cannot be reduced to machine-verifiable observations. Requiring all knowledge to present structured evidence can systematically exclude real but difficult-to-quantify harm. Traceability must admit testimony and dissent while marking their interpretive boundaries.
* Provisional Conclusion
Mature knowledge carries two forms of traceability: backward to sources, observations, assumptions, and inference; forward to judgment, authority, action, consequence, and new observation. The first disciplines warrant and the second disciplines responsibility. Together they place an output inside a history that can be challenged and revised.

Traceability is not maximized recording. It is the minimum chain sufficient to reconstruct reasons and consequences. Its quality is measured by whether it enables refutation, correction, stopping, and remedy—not by log volume.
* Promotion Criteria
Further tests should cover option exclusion, narrative evidence, minimal disclosure and less powerful participants' actual ability to correct premises. Logs should support reconsideration, not merely institutional self-certification.

Test at least three cases: reliable source with faulty inference, reasonable inference without authority, and successful execution with harmful long-term effects. Establish deletion rules for conflicts between privacy and answerability, and show that “not observed” never silently becomes “no effect.” Until these boundaries mature, this remains a knowledge discipline rather than a new Charter principle.
