* Principles

** Executable does not mean authorized

* Claim
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: charter-action-and-authority-en
:END:

Proposal, decision, authority, execution, and outcome remain distinguishable, auditable, and independently rejectable. Tool availability or invocation success does not create authority.

* Rationale

Real action changes resources, rights, and risk; explicit authority scope is the contract boundary connecting reasoning to external effects.

[[file:../30-reflections/30.60-action-requires-authority.org][source Reflection]] · [[file:../20-engineering/20.10-cross-repository-realization-map.org][cross-repository engineering map]]

* Counterarguments

Pre-authorized policy, capability tokens, and emergency stops can authorize action without a human click each time.

* Engineering Consequences

Execution entry points verify subject, resource, action, time, and environment; emit distinct execution and outcome receipts; support revocation or compensation.

* Failure Modes

A proposal executes directly; expired authority remains reusable; a return code is treated as the business outcome.

* Open Questions

How should distributed delegation compose? How are cross-organizational authority conflicts resolved?
