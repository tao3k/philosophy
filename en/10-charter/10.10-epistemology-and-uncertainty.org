* Claim
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: charter-epistemology-and-uncertainty-en
:END:

A system must not silently promote missing, conflicted, or hypothetical information into known fact. Decision-relevant unknown, conflicted, and hypothesized states retain provenance, scope, and resolution conditions.

* Rationale

A visible unknown frontier enables retrieval, measurement, pausing, and proportional assurance; hidden unknown lets fluency replace evidence.

[[file:../30-reflections/30.50-unknown-as-first-class.org][source Reflection]] · [[file:../20-engineering/20.10-cross-repository-realization-map.org][cross-repository engineering map]]

* Counterarguments

Low-risk defaults and explicit closed worlds remain useful, but their scope must be declared rather than presented as universal fact.

* Engineering Consequences

Public models express epistemic status; queries and receipts preserve provenance; admission includes missing-evidence and conflict negatives.

* Failure Modes

Status labels exist but execution ignores them; unknown is swallowed by empty strings, zero values, or model completion.

* Open Questions

Which boundaries deserve explicit modeling? How should unknown propagate without creating epistemic noise?
