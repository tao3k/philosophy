This note uses [[https://www.nber.org/papers/w35574][NBER Working Paper 35574]], a 2026 working paper not treated here as completed peer review.
* Context
The study asks a representative sample to seek spending and investing advice from LLMs and simulates lifetime consequences of following it. The abstract reports movement toward several life-cycle prescriptions alongside systematic variation by gender, AI experience, and financial literacy; gender differences arise from both prompts and model responses to otherwise identical prompts.
* Interpretation Boundaries
Moving toward a standard model is not identical to serving personal values, and simulated lifetime effects are not observed causal outcomes. Model, prompt, and 2026-time specificity constrain transfer; group variation is not automatically legitimate personalization.
* Influence
It shows how one answer can accumulate into a long trajectory, entering the value boundary of [[file:../30-reflections/30.92-stewardship-not-sovereignty.org][Stewardship]]: does AI help express ends or write a typical life into the future?
* Interpretation Chain
Survey prompts → LLM advice → life-cycle model → heterogeneous long-run effects → Tao3k questions about supply, demand, authority, and revisability.
