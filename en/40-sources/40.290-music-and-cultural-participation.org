* Context
The music survey used German items and recruitment through an electronics store, German universities, and other channels, not a global random sample; it relied on recall and self-report. The NEA report, prepared by Margaret E. Blume-Kohout, Sara R. Leonard, and Jennifer L. Novak-Leonard, concerns live visual and performing arts attendance.
* Interpretation Boundaries
The three dimensions neither classify universal personalities nor establish well-being gains from a listening approach. Historical US data cannot describe present-day China directly; reported barriers are not intervention results after removing them.

Neither source tests the essay's AI-supported listening, recommendations, or attendance scenarios. The claim that experience can matter in itself is this repository's philosophical argument, not a conclusion derived from survey associations.
* Influence
The essay asks separately why someone wants to participate, which conditions exist, and whether an experience is worth keeping. Course completion, dwell time, and recommendation clicks cannot answer for the person. This shapes a Reflection's questions rather than amending Principle or Governance.
* Interpretation Chain
Different reasons for listening → retain different ways in, including learning, enjoyment, and relationships, instead of prescribing an endpoint.

Interested people also report practical barriers → ask separately whether a suggestion is usable, rather than attribute nonparticipation to insufficient initiative.

These extensions guide an argument only. The imagined listener took part in neither study, and TAO3K acquires no evidence of improved lives from their existence.
