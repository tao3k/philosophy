This note uses the [[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/peirce/][Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy entry on Peirce]] to focus on fallibilism, continuing inquiry, and the methodological relation among abduction, deduction, and induction. It is a secondary interpretive entry, not a substitute for Peirce's primary texts.
* Context
Peircean fallibilism does not say that every belief is equally unreliable. It denies that inquiry at a particular historical moment may assume possession of final, unrevisable truth, while also rejecting skeptical paralysis. The entry connects this stance with the injunction not to block the way of inquiry and with a continuing process of hypothesis formation, consequence derivation, and testing.
* Interpretation Boundaries
Fallibilism does not imply indefinite deferral of decisions or permit absence of evidence to masquerade as openness. Action sometimes occurs under incomplete knowledge; the discipline is to retain uncertainty, revision conditions, and subsequent tests rather than refuse responsibility. The source does not prove any versioning, lineage, or invalidation implementation correct.
