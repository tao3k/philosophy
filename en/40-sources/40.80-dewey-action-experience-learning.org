This note uses the [[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dewey/][SEP entry on John Dewey]] to focus on experience as processual and transactional doing and undergoing by organisms in environments, and on reflection as a reorganization of subsequent activity.
* Context
Dewey rejects reducing experience to atomic sensations inside a subject. Experience includes action, undergoing, adjustment, and readjustment in an environment; known experience purposefully abstracts relations from direct experience. Philosophy thus has no privilege of final truth outside life but serves as equipment for living in a concrete world.
* Interpretation Boundaries
The source does not show that every feedback loop produces knowledge or authorize experiments on people who did not consent. Outcomes may be contaminated by noise, delay, and confounding. Connecting doing with undergoing still requires measurement, causal, and ethical judgment.
* Influence
It enters [[file:../30-reflections/30.90-knowing-after-action.org][Knowing Again After Action]], supporting the distinctions between execution receipt and outcome and the requirement that consequence alter knowledge state. It deepens practical learning without adding a Charter principle.
* Interpretation Chain
SEP entry → Deweyan experience and inquiry → action, undergoing consequence, and reflective reorganization → Tao3k observe–reconcile–learn loop. The engineering vocabulary is a modern mapping, not a system design found in Dewey's text.
