This note uses Lauren Ross and James Woodward's [[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causal-explanation-science/][SEP entry on causal approaches to scientific explanation]], taking Woodward's interventionist and counterfactual line as the present interpretive entry point.
* Context
Interventionism asks whether an explanation reveals the variables on which an outcome depends under appropriate changes or interventions, rather than merely identifying temporal sequence or correlation. The entry also discusses explanatory depth, variable grain, screening-off, and cross-level causation, showing why adding detail does not automatically improve explanation.
* Interpretation Boundaries
Not every variable can ethically be manipulated, and not every domain permits real experiments. An intervention model may choose the wrong variables, ignore structural constraints, or treat people as controllable objects. Interventionism alone cannot determine which action is authorized.
