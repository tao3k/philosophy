This note places the unity of knowledge and action in the Ming context of moral practice and criticism of empty discourse. The SEP [[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/wang-yangming/][Wang Yangming entry]] provides a current secondary check. No critical edition of the primary text is pinned, so this note has no authority for verbatim quotation.

[[file:../README.org][English navigation]] · [[file:../30-reflections/30.10-knowledge-action-in-agent-age.org][corresponding reflection]]

* Context

The historical concern is that moral understanding, motivation, cultivation, and practice cannot be separated for convenience. Wang criticizes treating knowledge and action as two tasks—first finish knowing, later decide whether to act—while allowing them to be discussed separately when correcting different failures. The point is not elimination of conceptual distinctions but refusal to call verbal assent genuine knowledge when it has no practical constraint.

For modern AI, the question is therefore not whether a model can recite a principle, but whether a judgment enters proposal, decision, authority, and action correctly and remains informed advice when authority is absent. “Knowing without authority” is coherent in a modern system; this is a necessary Tao3k revision of the historical doctrine.

* Interpretation Boundaries

It may inspire knowledge–action closure, but it does not imply that non-execution disproves knowledge and cannot justify unauthorized action. Ethical practice and distributed-system state machines are not the same object. Any protocol, runtime, or authority model still requires independent engineering evidence.

* Influence

It enters [[file:../30-reflections/30.10-knowledge-action-in-agent-age.org][Unity Without Collapse]], yielding the thesis that knowledge and action share one chain of responsibility while their engineering states remain distinct. Knowledge should constrain practice without bypassing human authority, proportional risk, or refusal.
