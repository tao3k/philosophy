* Context
The sources occupy three contexts that cannot substitute for one another.

The first concerns how rest is understood. Personal accounts provide experiential openings; the analysis
of doctoral students’ rest shame places experience within self, technical, and institutional discipline;
consumer and social psychology studies test how waste beliefs and precise scheduling relate to experience.
Together they resist reducing rest to recovery for work, but do not prove one ideal form of rest.

The second concerns time after AI enters work. Random assignment in the NBER working paper supports a
specific average-effect claim in participating firms, including the important non-result that task
composition did not measurably change. The Berkeley ethnography can see pace, concurrent work, and
self-expansion of responsibility, but it is not randomized and remains in progress. One shows that relief
can occur; the other shows how capability can be converted by people and organizations into more work.
The divergence is itself a result requiring explanation.

The third returns time to social and material surroundings. The boundary survey shows that apparent
leisure may remain on call. Rosa argues that meaningful response cannot be produced to order. Park and
architecture materials show that without accessible places to remain without constant consumption, time
does not readily become participation. The Ditan “sea” adds that beauty is not merely delivered by a
designer or recommender; ordinary attention can make it.
* Interpretation Boundaries
This synthesis does not prove that more rest is always better, that less planning is always better, or
that quiet, solitude, and slow life are universal answers. It must not romanticize gaps created by job
loss, poverty, illness, or exclusion as “room.” Continuous time, rough scheduling, and public seating mean
different things under different care obligations, bodily conditions, cultures, and relationships.

Nor may the two AI studies be collapsed into a net-effect number. Their tools, organizations, methods,
adoption, and measures differ. Two fewer weekly email hours do not equal two net hours of free life; denser
work does not prove that every participant rejects the effort. The sources support branching hypotheses
and tests, not a verdict that AI necessarily liberates or accelerates.

Vendor, media, government, and prize materials select and frame what they present. A link verifies a public
claim; it is not independent acceptance of real-world effect. Any product or governance claim still needs
participant experience, counterexamples, revocable consent, and observation of displaced burdens.
* Influence
These sources do not directly add a Charter Principle. They make Reflection questions more precise: from
“how much time was saved?” to duration, continuity, on-call boundaries, refusal, burden distribution, and
authority over use; from “what activity should be recommended?” to conditions in which experience may
occur; and from “AI can do more” to whether capability expansion permits a person to carry less.

They also constrain [[file:../30-reflections/30.140-leaving-room-for-life.org][Leaving Room for Life]], [[file:../30-reflections/30.150-beyond-the-bill.org][Beyond the Bill]], [[file:../30-reflections/30.160-beauty-in-everyday-life.org][Beauty in Everyday Life]], and
[[file:../30-reflections/30.170-when-less-use-is-good.org][When Less Use Is Good]]: a bill cannot decide what matters, a plan cannot exhaust an experience, beauty
cannot be standardized by a platform, and reduced use cannot be declared successful without the person’s
account.
* Interpretation Chain
Personal rest narratives + analysis of rest shame + studies of leisure belief and scheduling
→ free time can remain governed by the scale of production; rest need not be justified by future output.

Boundary survey + randomized AI experiment + AI ethnography
→ calendar blankness, automation savings, and disposable time are different quantities; AI’s consequences
depend on how organizations allocate added capability.

Rosa interview + Changshou Park + Liu Jiakun citation + the Ditan practice of seeing
→ experiences cannot be booked, but they require approachable places and conditions in which people may
remain, participate, and alter a shared world.

All three chains enter [[file:../30-reflections/30.210-why-freed-time-does-not-return.org][Why Did Freed Time Not Return to Life?]]. The move from sources to protected
continuous time, refusal of further demands, and freedom from proving meaning is Tao3k’s modern synthesis.
It belongs to no single source and has not been admitted by Governance as a Principle.
