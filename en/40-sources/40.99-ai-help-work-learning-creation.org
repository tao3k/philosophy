This note synthesizes two conceptual sources and five empirical strands: J. C. R. Licklider's [[https://groups.csail.mit.edu/medg/people/psz/Licklider][Man-Computer Symbiosis]] (1960); Douglas Engelbart's [[https://dougengelbart.org/content/view/138/][Augmenting Human Intellect]] (1962); Brynjolfsson, Li and Raymond's [[https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161][Generative AI at Work]] (2023 working paper; 2025 QJE version); Bastani et al.'s [[https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122][Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning]] (2025); Vaccaro, Almaatouq and Malone's [[https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-024-02024-1][meta-analysis of human-AI combinations]] (2024); Doshi and Hauser's [[https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290][generative-AI short-story experiment]] (2024); and the ILO [[https://www.ilo.org/publications/generative-ai-and-jobs-refined-global-index-occupational-exposure][2025 occupational exposure index]].
* Context
Licklider wanted computers to enter problem formulation and real-time thought instead of merely solving preformulated problems. Engelbart used “augmentation” for a person together with language, artifacts, methods and training. That is a source's historical term, not this repository's definition of a person. Both wrote in contexts whose division of human and machine abilities is not a permanent allocation for the present.

The empirical studies show that “AI assistance” has heterogeneous outcomes that cannot be summarized by one performance gain.

#+NAME: ai-help-evidence-boundaries-en
| Source | Observation within the particular study | What it does not establish |
|--------+-----------------------------------------+----------------------------|
| Customer-support study | Among 5,179 agents, productivity rose about 14% on average and about 34% for novice and lower-skilled workers, with suggestive evidence of knowledge diffusion | Every occupation and worker will learn or share benefits fairly |
| High-school mathematics experiment | Ordinary GPT assistance improved supported practice while unassisted learning could worsen; a teacher-designed guarded version mitigated the problem | AI necessarily makes people less capable, or one prompt design generalizes to all education |
| Meta-analysis of 106 experiments | Human-AI combinations outperformed humans alone on average but underperformed the better of human or AI alone; task type and relative ability mattered | A human final confirmation automatically creates synergy |
| Short-story experiment | AI ideas improved average creativity, writing and enjoyment ratings, especially for lower-baseline writers, while stories also became more similar | Every creative practice or culture will improve or converge in the same way |
| ILO occupational index | About one in four workers globally are in occupations with some GenAI exposure, with job transformation judged the more likely aggregate effect | Job security, distribution of income or any individual's future has been determined |

The studies measure different objects: task speed, unassisted exams, experimental performance, ratings of work and occupational task exposure. Adding them into a “total human progress score” would be meaningless.
* Interpretation Boundaries
The conceptual sources do not prove that today's AI improves human lives. The empirical sources cannot generalize local outcomes to every person, tool or long-term life. Customer-support productivity is not human worth, a mathematics exam is not all learning, laboratory synergy is not organizational responsibility and story ratings are not the creator's whole experience.

The ILO index measures potential exposure rather than forecasting unemployment. An aggregate judgment that transformation is more likely guarantees neither safety nor displacement for a particular person.

“Human alone” in experiments often assumes a person without ordinary tools. For someone needing continuing assistance, an unaided baseline may be the wrong normative aim. Conversely, legitimate dependence must not hide provider lock-in, reduced ability to understand or one-way extraction of experience.
* Influence
This repository observes at least three things separately: task completion, change in understanding or action according to a person's purpose, and whether the person can still direct important affairs. A human in the loop, an explanation or improved assisted performance cannot separately establish all three.

Help should fit the person's purpose. Delegation should leave less to worry about; learning should ask whether judgment remains when help changes or disappears; creative help should ask whether people enjoyed taking part and whether expression remains diverse. We must also ask who receives saved time and labor, and who spends time learning a new tool, finding mistakes and repairing harm.
* Interpretation Chain
Licklider's problem formulation and Engelbart's whole capability system → differentiated evidence from work, education, collaboration, creation and occupational exposure → [[file:../30-reflections/30.99-shanjia-yuwu.org][Skillful Use of Things]] distinguishes completion, growth and direction → tests through [[file:../30-reflections/30.90-knowing-after-action.org][learning after action]] and [[file:../30-reflections/30.97-shared-lives-and-responsibility.org][distribution of benefits and burdens]]. The last two steps are Tao3k's normative interpretation, not a shared conclusion of the source authors.
