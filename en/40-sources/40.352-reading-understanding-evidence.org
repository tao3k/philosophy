We examined designs, main results and relevant limitations, without reproducing analyses or auditing every supplement. The two 2025 items are identified as the arXiv v1 preprints read; the others are a DeTermIt 2024 workshop paper and a CSCW 2023 paper. Model names refer to experimental versions, not every current model.

* Context

The first two studies ask whether simplification improves on an original and whether generated summaries match human plain-language summaries, respectively. Their different directions do not cancel one another out. The third addresses public information; the fourth addresses explanation and verification. Together they help formulate questions, not replicate one intervention across settings.

We distinguish subjective appraisal, understanding within a specified task and consequences in actual practice. Evidence on text reading also cannot automatically establish effects in code maintenance, research collaboration or reading Chinese classics.
* Interpretation Boundaries

Readability scores do not directly measure understanding. Preference and confidence still matter, but should not replace other evidence. We infer neither that more demanding interfaces are better nor that every AI interaction should become an examination.

This is not a systematic review and claims neither exhaustive coverage, absence of bias nor universal effectiveness. These materials support bounded comparisons; other languages, reading needs and practical consequences require separate observation.

Making choices and routes of appeal intelligible is our normative question, not an outcome established by the government-letter study. Research findings, modern interpretation and shared principles remain distinct.
* Influence

The essay moves from outputs resembling human prose towards what readers can now distinguish, while retaining simplification's positive possibilities. Responsibility does not fall entirely on readers, nor can producers establish benefit by scoring themselves. This round changes no Principle, Governance or engineering acceptance requirement.
* Interpretation Chain

Different controls and reading tasks → distinctions between readability, understanding and verification → the essay's proposal to compare accounts against real purposes and watch for missing conditions.

This interpretation enters a conversation with [[file:40.351-bai-juyi-clarity-and-credibility.org][Bai Juyi's poetic aims]] and [[file:40.350-feynman-explanation-and-teaching.org][Feynman's explanation and self-questioning]], not an experiment spanning centuries. Contract checks establish document conformance, not whether readers understand.

If simplification makes compliance faster, did it also reveal when a person need not comply? Expression can serve a publisher without equally serving a reader. The [[file:../30-reflections/30.350-making-difficult-ideas-clear.org][essay]] therefore distinguishes understanding from agreement and keeps room for questions, refusal and pause.

Readers' time is also a real cost. Demanding lengthy tests from everyone to demonstrate reliable explanation can recreate exclusion. We want to observe a few distinctions consequential to the actual matter, while allowing trustworthy delegation. An average score alone cannot settle the balance.
