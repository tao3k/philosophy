This note uses the [[https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pseudo-science/][SEP entry Science and Pseudo-Science]] to record the place of Popperian falsifiability in the demarcation problem and the criticisms that followed. It is not a summary of Popper's complete philosophy of science.
* Context
Popper rejected verifiability as the boundary of empirical science and required scientific statements to be capable of logical conflict with possible observations. The entry also explains that falsifiability is not a complete definition of scientific practice: a single demarcation criterion can exclude legitimate science or admit pseudoscience, and the methodological willingness to accept refutation matters alongside logical form.
* Interpretation Boundaries
Falsifiability primarily disciplines empirical statements. It cannot be directly imposed on normative principles such as dignity, agency, or proportional assurance. Nor does one anomaly necessarily overturn a theory without examining measurement, background assumptions, and alternatives. Tao3k therefore rejects the slogan that all content must be falsifiable.
* Influence
It supports requiring empirical claims to expose possible observations that would count against them. It enters [[file:../30-reflections/30.80-refutable-challengeable-revisable.org][Refutable, Challengeable, Revisable]], where empirical, causal, and normative forms of authority are distinguished.
* Interpretation Chain
SEP entry → Popperian demarcation and falsifiability → criticism of the criterion's limits → Tao3k failure conditions for empirical claims. The normative challenge path is a further synthesis of this repository, not a doctrine attributed back to Popper.
